33. Twist number one. Paying cash didn't beat the loan. Why?

34. Because Max's loan costs seven percent, and the money he kept invested earned seven percent. They cancel out.

35. So the real rule is simple. If your loan rate is lower than what your money can earn, financing wins. If it's higher, paying cash wins.

44. Nora had the same value loss and repairs, and no interest. But her fifty thousand dollars wasn't invested and growing for her. That hidden cost is why she ended up level with Max.

45. Leasing can make sense when a manufacturer offers a really cheap lease deal, when you'd swap your car every few years anyway, or when you drive very little.

46. But watch for the traps. Most leases limit your miles, often around ten to twelve thousand a year, and charge you for every mile over.

47. You can also pay for wear and tear when you hand it back. And ending a lease early can be very expensive.

50. Second, keep your car longer. The biggest drop in value happens in the first few years. After that, every extra year is cheap.

51. And third, negotiate the price of the car, not the monthly payment. A low payment can hide a much longer loan.
